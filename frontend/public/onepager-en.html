The gap

Music schools teach the practical craft — tuning, chords, timing, reading, playing by ear. But the software that exists sits at two extremes: consumer apps built for solo gamified practice, or abstract theory drills. Almost nothing is built for how a teacher actually works on the hands‑and‑ears skills, or lets a student get honest feedback on their playing between lessons.

What it is

Four practical tools, one browser tab — working on the devices schools already have:

01 Tuner

Accurate real‑time pitch from the mic, down to the low E. Tune by the needle — or hide it and tune by ear against a played reference.

02 Chord Detector

Strum a chord and it names it and draws the shape — real polyphonic detection that ignores the string's overtones.

03 Reading & ear trainer

A practice game that listens to what you play and builds note‑reading and pitch accuracy, one bar at a time, at your own tempo.

04 Progression builder

Pick a key and a common progression, hear it play back, loop it, tweak it, and save it — turning theory into something you can actually play along to.

No install, browser‑based, mic‑powered. Works on a school's Chromebooks, iPads and laptops as‑is.

Why now, and why me

I'm a working musician. I built the first version as a tool for my own practice — and it worked: my sight‑reading is already noticeably better. That's the whole thesis. The tools I wanted didn't exist with this practical functionality, so I made them.

"I built this to fix my own practice — and my note‑reading is already much better." — Founder, musician & builder

Who it's for

  • Music schools & their teachers — practical tools for lessons and for setting home practice.
  • Students — honest, real‑time feedback on tuning, chords, reading and timing between lessons.
  • The teacher who wants to see that practice actually happened — the foundation for progress tracking.

The ask — a summer demo, then a pilot

Teachers are flexible over the summer break — the right moment to gather a handful of school leaders for a hands‑on session, learn what they need, and line up a small pilot. What we most want to hear from them:

  • What do students actually struggle to practice at home?
  • What can a teacher not see about a student's practice between lessons?
  • If this existed, who decides to buy — the teacher, the school, the parent?

How a pilot would work

Low commitment, no cost — built to learn, not to sell.

  1. 1

    One to three schools and a handful of teachers who actually want in.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

    At the end we decide whether it's worth paying for, and what a school version needs next — student accounts, progress tracking, and the rest.

No cost, no lock‑in, and a direct line to the person building it.
